> **Input from all prior:** every function has incumbents (mostly enterprise, mostly top-tier). **This section's job:** map the stack, find **mid-market India gaps, integration/data/AI layers, and services-to-platform** wedges.

## 15.2 The structural insight
Global incumbents are **enterprise-priced, heavy, slow to deploy, and built for Tier-1 multinationals**. India has **thousands of mid-market** manufacturers/exporters/CROs/distributors that are **too small for Veeva, too regulated for generic SaaS**. This is a *durable, fragmented, underserved* market — the recurring whitespace across every section.

## 15.3 Money & power
IT budgets concentrated at top tier; mid-market buys reluctantly, needs ROI + validation + low TCO. QA/IT can block. Switching costs high once embedded → durable revenue.

## 15.4 Manual workflows & pain
Disconnected point tools + Excel + email bridging them; no integration layer; data trapped per-silo; AI absent.

## 15.5 Opportunity harvest
- **🟢 SaaS:** India-fit mid-market modules (eQMS, RIM, digital-ops, eClinical).
- **🟢 Integration/data layer:** connect silos (QMS↔MES↔LIMS↔RIM) + build a data fabric.
- **🟢 AI layer:** AI on top of existing systems (dossier, deviation, PV, documents).
- **🟢 Services→platform:** implementation/validation services that climb to product.

## 15.6 Scored opportunity (condensed)

Market 8 · Severity 7 · Founder fit 8 · Defensibility 8 (integrations+data). **Attractiveness ≈ 70 / Risk ≈ 50 → Medium–High; Platform.** Could become the connective tissue across your other products.

## 15.7 Weak opportunities to avoid
Head-on enterprise suite vs. Veeva/SAP; one-off custom-dev shop with no product.

## 15.8 Customer-discovery questions
- **CIO/IT head:** Which systems are stitched by Excel/email? What can't you afford from global vendors? Integration pain? AI appetite + data-governance constraints?
