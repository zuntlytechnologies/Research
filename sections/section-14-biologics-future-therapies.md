# Section 14 — Biologics & Future Therapies

> **Input from S1/S8:** value migrates from small molecules to large molecules. **This section's job:** identify future billion-dollar spaces and **clearly separate scientist-led cores from non-scientist enabling layers.**

## 14.1 The landscape
- **Monoclonal antibodies (mAbs)** & **biosimilars** (copies of off-patent biologics — *similar*, not identical; require clinical comparability). India (Biocon, Dr. Reddy's, Zydus) is a biosimilar contender.
- **Cell therapy** (e.g., CAR-T), **gene therapy**, **mRNA**, **precision/personalized medicine**, **synthetic biology**.
- **Reality:** science-heavy, capital-heavy (C4–C5), cold-chain-critical, manufacturing-complex, slow. India is emerging, government-backed (e.g., indigenous CAR-T approvals).

## 14.2 Money & power
Innovators + scientific founders + deep capital (PE/VC/strategics) hold power. Manufacturing/cold-chain/quality complexity is extreme. Margins high on winners.

## 14.3 Founder-fit separation (critical)
| Layer | Founder entry |
|---|---|

## 14.4 Manual workflows & pain
Ultra-cold-chain monitoring; chain-of-identity/custody for cell therapy (patient-specific!); complex comparability data; high-value product security.

## 14.5 Opportunity harvest
- **🟢 Cold chain/IoT:** ultra-cold-chain monitoring + chain-of-custody (premium, mandatory).
- **🟢 Serialization/security:** high-value biologic authentication.
- **🟡 Data:** RWE & safety for biosimilars (S19).
- **🟡 CDMO enablement:** biologics capacity discovery (S22).

## 14.6 Scored opportunity (condensed)
**OPP-28 — Chain-of-custody & ultra-cold-chain for biologics/cell therapy.** HW+SaaS+compliance. Capital C3, TTF T3. Market 7 (growing) · Severity 9 · Founder fit 7 · Defensibility 8. **Attractiveness ≈ 68 / Risk ≈ 55 → Medium–High; long-term strategic.** Rides the biologics wave without doing the science.

## 14.7 Weak opportunities to avoid
