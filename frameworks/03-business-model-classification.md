# 03 — Business-Model Classification Framework

> **Purpose.** Give every opportunity a precise business-model identity so we can compare apples to apples, understand its economics, and judge its compounding potential. A pharma opportunity is rarely "just SaaS." Most durable pharma-industry companies are **hybrids** that layer software, data, services, and sometimes hardware.

---

## Part B — Mandatory questions to classify each opportunity

For every opportunity, answer explicitly:

1. **Primary model identity** — Is this *software-only, hardware-enabled, services-led, infrastructure-led, marketplace-led, export-led, fintech-led, or hybrid*?
2. **Non-scientist entry** — Can a non-scientist founder enter? (Yes / Yes-with-partner / No)
3. **Expertise to hire/partner** — What domain expertise must be hired or partnered for (e.g., RA head, QA head, analytical chemist, customs/DGFT expert, lab director)?
4. **Minimum viable entry point** — The smallest credible v1 that earns trust and revenue.
5. **Sub-₹5 cr feasibility** — Can it start under ₹5 crore? If not, **what phased version can**?
6. **Time to first revenue** (use `05` timeline bands)
7. **Time to meaningful scale**
8. **Horizon** — short-term / medium-term / long-term / lifetime-scale
9. **Cash-flow vs. venture vs. strategic-acquisition vs. infra-compounding**
10. **Platform potential** — Can it become a platform over time, and via which ladder rungs (`02`, Part D)?

## Part C — Revenue-architecture cheat sheet (how money is actually made)

- **Subscription (SaaS):** ARR, NRR, low churn target <10%/yr; pharma sells slowly but stays long.
- **Usage/outcome (AI):** per-document, per-case, per-batch, per-dossier; align price to a cost the customer already pays.
- **Per-FTE / per-seat managed services:** classic India arbitrage; protect with process IP + software.
- **Take rate (marketplace):** 2–15% depending on value-add; logistics-heavy = thin, intelligence-heavy = thick.
- **Spread (fintech):** interest/discount spread; needs cheap capital + low default underwriting.
- **Device + recurring (IoT/HW):** sell device near-cost, monetize connectivity/SaaS/consumables (razor-blade).
- **Consumable per-unit (packaging/serialization):** fractions of a paisa per pack × billions of packs = real money at scale.
- **Retainer + success fee (services/export/tender):** de-risks the customer, captures upside.

## Part D — Hybrid stacking patterns that win in pharma

The strongest founder-fit companies **stack layers**:

- **Services → Data → SaaS:** Do the work manually first (services), accumulate proprietary data, then sell the data + software. *(e.g., export registration intelligence, tender intelligence.)*
- **Hardware → Data → SaaS:** Deploy sensors/devices (cold chain, serialization scanners), monetize the data stream and analytics. *(razor-blade + data network.)*
- **Marketplace → Fintech:** Build transaction flow first, then finance the transactions (working capital on top of the marketplace).
- **Compliance services → Managed compliance → Compliance platform:** Audit readiness as a service → continuous managed compliance → multi-tenant QMS/RegTech.
- **Distribution/logistics → Data/Fintech:** Move goods → own the demand/credit data → finance the channel.

---

## Part E — Red flags by model

- **Pure SaaS in deep-regulated GxP core (QMS/MES/LIMS) vs. global incumbents:** hard to displace head-on; enter mid-market or adjacent.
- **Marketplace without a wedge service:** liquidity is brutal; pharma buyers distrust pure listings. Pair with a service or data hook.
- **Hardware without recurring revenue:** one-time sales, support burden, commoditization. Always attach SaaS/consumable.
- **Fintech without proprietary data or cheap capital:** you're a worse bank. Need an origination wedge (marketplace/ERP/distributor data).
- **Services with no climb path:** fine as cash-flow, but be honest it won't compound to platform value.
