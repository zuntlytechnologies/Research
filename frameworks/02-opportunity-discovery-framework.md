# 02 — Opportunity-Discovery Framework

> **Purpose.** A repeatable engine for *finding*, *interrogating*, and *qualifying* pharma opportunities — so we don't fall in love with ideas, and we don't reject good non-SaaS ideas just because they aren't pure software. This framework is applied inside every section's "Opportunity harvest" step.

---

## Part A — The discovery engine (how we generate candidates)

We hunt for opportunities along **seven signal lines**. Each is a reliable place where money and pain accumulate.

### Signal line 1 — Manual-workflow detection ("Excel-to-X" mode)
Wherever the industry runs on **Excel, email, PDFs, shared drives, WhatsApp, paper registers, printed labels, physical samples, manual counts, or field operations**, there is latent software/services/hardware value. For each detected workflow, capture:

- Current process → Pain points → Users → Buyer/budget owner → Existing tools → Gaps → SaaS / AI / Hardware / IoT / Managed-services / Infra opportunity → Adoption difficulty.

> ⚠️ **Scope correction (explicit):** Manual-workflow detection is *one* signal line, not the whole framework. Do **not** collapse every opportunity into "SaaS to replace an Excel sheet." Many of the most defensible pharma businesses are services, infrastructure, hardware, marketplace, or fintech.

### Signal line 2 — Money-flow friction
Follow the rupee. Wherever payment is slow, credit is expensive, margins are thin-but-contested, or working capital is trapped (distributor credit cycles, export receivables, MSME API suppliers), there is **fintech / financing / procurement** value.

### Signal line 3 — Power asymmetry
Wherever one party controls a chokepoint (regulator approvals, channel power of stockists, KOL influence, big-buyer tenders), there is room for **intelligence, disintermediation, or enablement** for the weaker party.

### Signal line 4 — Regulatory mandate
New or tightening mandates (Schedule M revision, serialization/track-and-trace, DSCSA-style rules in export markets, data integrity, ESG/ZLD for APIs) **force** spend on a deadline. Mandate-driven demand is the most reliable B2B pharma demand.

### Signal line 5 — Physical/operational reality
Wherever there's a sample, a label, a cold box, a logbook, a calibration sticker, a warehouse count, or a field rep — software alone can't win. These are **hardware-enabled / IoT / managed-operations / hybrid** opportunities.

### Signal line 6 — Data exhaust & silos
Wherever valuable data is generated but trapped (tender results, export registrations, batch records, PV cases, channel sell-out, price/tender history), there is a **data-intelligence / data-network / marketplace** opportunity that compounds.

### Signal line 7 — Fragmentation & sub-scale buyers
India's mid-market pharma (thousands of small/mid manufacturers, exporters, distributors, labs) is **underserved by global enterprise vendors** (too expensive, too heavy) and overserved by nothing. This whitespace is a recurring source of platform opportunities.

## Part B — The interrogation checklist (per candidate)

For **every** opportunity that survives Part A, answer all of the following. Incomplete answers = not yet qualified.

**Problem & demand**
- Existing problem (stated in the customer's own words)
- Is it *mission-critical* or *nice-to-have*?
- Frequency × Severity (daily-painful vs. annual-annoyance)
- Why do customers still complain? Why haven't incumbents solved it?

**Customer & buyer**
- Customer (the company/segment) · User (who touches it) · Buyer (who signs) · Budget owner · Economic decision-maker · Who can *block* adoption (often QA or IT or a powerful distributor)

**Existing landscape**
- Existing vendors / solutions / substitutes (including "do nothing" and "a consultant + Excel")
- Why current solutions are inadequate (cost, fit, heaviness, India-unfit, no support)

**Why a startup wins / loses**
- Why a startup might succeed (wedge, focus, India-fit, hybrid model)
- Why a startup might fail (no budget, regulated inertia, channel power, capital wall, science gap)

**Go-to-market & friction**
- Buying process · Sales cycle length · Implementation difficulty · Integration complexity · Change-management need · Validation/qualification requirement (CSV, GxP) · Vendor-trust requirement

**Capital & capability**
- Capital requirement (use `04`/`05` bands) · Scientific expertise requirement · Partnership requirement · Operational complexity

**Verdict inputs**
- Repeatable sales motion? · Can it become defensible? · Can it scale beyond services? · Can it compound over 10–20 years? · Realistic founder entry point?

## Part C — The "is this even worth solving?" gate

Never assume a problem is worth solving. It must pass **all five** to proceed to scoring:

1. **Care** — Customers genuinely care (not just "interesting").
2. **Budget** — A budget line exists or can be created (or it saves a bigger, visible cost).
3. **Urgency** — There is a reason to act *now* (mandate, audit, loss, competitor move).
4. **Implementable** — The customer can actually adopt it given their IT/ops/regulatory constraints.
5. **Changeable** — The org can absorb the change (people, SOPs, validation).

A "yes-yes-yes-no-no" idea is a *future* idea, not a *now* idea — file it under medium/long-term, don't kill it.

## Part D — Services-to-platform ladder (don't reject "just services")

A founder with no science can legitimately *start with services* and climb. Map every opportunity onto this ladder and note the realistic climb path:

| Stage | What you sell | Margin profile | Moat building |
|-------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| 1. **Consulting** | Expert hours | Low-mid, non-scalable | Reputation, relationships, domain learning |
| 2. **Services** | Outcome delivery (e.g., dossier prep) | Mid, people-leveraged | Process IP, repeatability |
| 3. **Managed services** | Ongoing operations (e.g., PV case processing, audit readiness) | Mid-high, sticky | Embedded in customer ops, SLAs |
| 4. **Workflow platform** | Internal tooling productized | High, scalable | Process encoded in software |
| 5. **SaaS** | Self-serve / multi-tenant software | High, scalable | Switching cost, integrations |
| 6. **AI platform** | Software that *does the work* | Very high | Proprietary data + workflow lock-in |
| 7. **Data network / marketplace / infra** | Network effects, data exhaust | Highest, durable | Network effects, data moat, infra lock-in |

> **Rule:** Services is a *legitimate beachhead*, not a failure mode — **if** there is a credible path to Stage 4+. Services with no climb path = lifestyle business (fine, but classify it honestly as "cash-flow," not "platform").

## Part E — Opportunity-type classification (don't force-fit SaaS)

Tag each opportunity with one or more **types** (these are *not* mutually exclusive):

- Fast cash-flow opportunity
- Services-to-software opportunity
- Hardware-enabled opportunity
- IoT/device opportunity
- Infrastructure compounding opportunity
- Marketplace opportunity
- Export-enablement opportunity
- Pharma fintech opportunity
- Long-term strategic opportunity
- Venture-scale opportunity
- Acquisition-style (roll-up/buy-and-build) opportunity
- **Avoid** (with reason)

---

## Part F — Reasons to reject (and say why)

Reject, but always *document the reason* so we learn the pattern:

- Poor unit economics / structurally thin margins with no leverage path
- Weak or imaginary customer demand ("vitamin, not painkiller")
- Highly commoditized with price-only competition
- Unrealistic capital wall with no phased entry
- No defensible moat (easily copied, no data/network/switching cost)
- Depends *only* on regulatory arbitrage (rules change → business dies)
- Requires scientific credibility that **cannot** be hired, partnered, or phased in
- No practical founder entry path for this profile

> ❗ **Two anti-patterns to resist:** (1) Rejecting an idea *only because it isn't SaaS*. (2) Rejecting an idea *only because payback > 24 months*. Instead, **classify** it correctly (Part E) and **score** it (`05`).

## Part G — Customer-validation mode (built into every opportunity)

For each major opportunity, generate role-specific interview questions for the relevant subset of:
CEO · QA Head · QC Head · Regulatory Affairs Head · Export Head · Plant Head · Manufacturing Head · Supply Chain Head · CFO · Medical Affairs Head · Procurement Head · Distribution Head · Warehouse Head · Logistics Partner · C&F Agent · Stockist/Distributor · Hospital Pharmacy Head · Retail Pharmacy Owner · CDMO Head · CRO Head.

Questions must validate: **problem severity · budget availability · existing solutions · buying process · switching barriers · implementation challenges · regulatory concerns · ROI expectations · operational willingness · hardware/infra adoption readiness · vendor-trust requirements · procurement process · who can block adoption.**

A reusable question bank is maintained in each section; a master template lives in `04-founder-fit-framework.md` (validation section).

---

## Part H — Output of discovery

Each qualified opportunity becomes a **portfolio row** (`06-top-100-portfolio.md`) carrying: problem, customer, segment, existing solutions, business-model category, TAM estimate, regulatory complexity, capital band, time-to-first-revenue, time-to-scale, applicability scores (AI/SaaS/HW/Infra/Services/Export/Marketplace), competitive intensity, moat potential, founder fit, expertise required, partnership required, overall attractiveness, recommended priority, opportunity type.
